# Test the frozen candidate against stronger baselines

Keep the candidate unchanged while testing whether its earlier gains survive stronger comparisons. The cost is a small configuration search for each baseline family, three stochastic fits of the selected pipelines, and additional uncertainty calculations. This study measures model quality and repeatability. It does not measure serving latency or establish novelty.

The earlier comparisons used frozen reference configurations. Their results motivated the candidate, but they did not show that it outperforms appropriately tuned native models. The new study gives each baseline family four prespecified configurations, a common tree cap and the same low-false-positive validation selection criterion. Native category handling is retained where supported by the chosen baseline implementation. Equal configuration counts do not imply equal model capacity or equal training time.

Only training and validation rows enter configuration and tree-prefix selection. A record of the choices is written before calibration and final scoring. Source discovery reads full files for chronology, label-state and source-integrity audits; it does not turn those discovery counts into model-selection criteria. Training transformations are fitted on training rows only.

The repeat fits use the same cohorts with different random seeds. They reveal stochastic fitting and validation-prefix variation. They do not constitute new datasets or rolling-origin backtests. Later test windows are also evaluated using the same globally calibrated cutoff, without selecting a different cutoff for each period.

The paired uncertainty checks resample clusters shared by the candidate and a reference model. They preserve the comparison on the same cases, conditional on the fitted models and their calibration cutoffs. They omit uncertainty from training, hyperparameter selection and calibration. Temporal blocks can retain some local dependence while leaving cross-block dependence unresolved. Card and weekly checks on the simulated card data are separate checks; neither accounts for both grouping structures jointly. A screen requiring at least twenty clusters prevents reporting those intervals on the coarsest sources, but it is not a theorem guaranteeing valid coverage above that threshold.

The three synthetic sources remain consumed development evidence. A real Bitcoin dataset adds a distinct transfer test. The [original Elliptic paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02591) describes an illicit-transaction classification task, which differs from card or mobile-payment fraud. Unknown labels are excluded rather than counted as normal. The [publisher's feature description](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ellipticco/elliptic-data-set) distinguishes local features from neighbour aggregates that include forward information. The test uses the local positions excluding time and omits those explicit neighbour aggregates. Anonymous local features and provider normalization still prevent an independent audit of full point-in-time availability.

This is therefore real-data transfer evidence on a known-label subset. It cannot replace untouched payment-fraud confirmation. The previously requested competition download still returned an access error. No competition rules were accepted automatically and no access restriction was bypassed.

The research claim also needs care. [Narasimhan and Agarwal](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/narasimhan13.html) already optimize partial ROC area over selected false-positive ranges. [Zhu and colleagues](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/zhu22g.html) connect partial-AUC objectives with robust optimization. [Sagawa and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731) study worst-group training and regularization. These sources establish overlap with the ingredients of the candidate. They do not establish that the exact implementation is identical, or that its combination is unique. A distinct objective, a restricted tree optimizer and checked empirical descent must be compared directly with the closest methods before a novelty claim.

The real Bitcoin paper also includes random-forest and graph-model comparisons. This study compares the selected boosted-model families under a different, local-feature-only protocol. Its scores should not be presented as a reproduction of that paper or as a global state-of-the-art result.

## For whoever builds this

Skip this section unless you are running or reviewing the study.

The frozen study plan is `validation-study/prespecified-plan.json`. The runner is `experiment/validation_experiment.py`, with baseline construction and prefix selection in `experiment/tuned_models.py`. The data adapter is `experiment/validation_data.py`. Paired cluster calculations are in `experiment/cluster_uncertainty.py`; individual and combined reports are generated by `experiment/validation_report.py`. Private self-contained jobs are packaged by `experiment/validation_job.py`.

The model families are frozen V6, native categorical CatBoost, one-hot-input XGBoost and native categorical LightGBM. Each baseline receives four configurations on primary seed 17. The selected hyperparameters are then reused on seeds 42 and 73; tree prefixes are reselected on validation for those repeats. All baseline tree prefixes are considered. V6 keeps its existing stopping rule and numerical parameters. The cap is 100 trees, with at most 150,000 sampled training rows and 100,000 rows in each later partition. Sampling is label-blind and fixed at seed 20261112. There is no new V6 hyperparameter search.

The declared calibration targets are 0.0005, 0.001 and 0.01. A primary result is seed 17; repeat ranges are not pooled positive-case counts. Bootstrap calculations use 2,000 paired draws with seed 20261112 and require at least twenty observed clusters. PaySim uses four-step temporal blocks. Sparkov uses calendar weeks and card IDs separately. BAF uses its two observed final months. Elliptic uses its eight observed final time steps. The latter two sources cannot satisfy the cluster screen. Ordinary row-level beta intervals in the result file assume independent rows and must not substitute for unsupported cluster checks.

The Elliptic source is `ellipticco/elliptic-data-set`, under `CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0`. It has a feature CSV without a header and a separate class CSV. Transaction IDs join one-to-one and remain internal. Published class 1 maps to positive, class 2 to negative, and unknown rows are excluded. The predictor allowlist is feature positions 2 through 94 inclusive, giving 93 anonymous local numeric predictors. Position 0 is the join ID, position 1 is the time step, and positions 95 through 166 are the excluded neighbour aggregates. The time edges are derived from source timestamp groups before known-label filtering. No modified dataset or raw feature rows are exported.

Discovery scripts and reports record source file hashes, source and known-label counts, allowed columns, chronology, missingness, time groups and cohort hashes. The original synthetic source files and sampled cohorts were checked against prior evidence. Elliptic discovery was repeated with different CSV chunk sizes. All proposed training time groups contained both classes. The frozen candidate's primary synthetic results must reproduce the preceding study before changes in the baseline comparison are interpreted.

The selection record is `selection.json`, written before final scoring. Every baseline trial remains in `trials.csv`; all budgets, seeds and regressions remain in `results.csv`. Repeat prefixes and fit durations are in `models.json`. Global-cutoff window results are in `window-results.csv`. Supported and unsupported paired checks are in `uncertainty.json`. Reports must retain prefix-zero cases and distinguish the real AML transfer task from the synthetic sources.

The test suites cover source ID alignment, unknown-label exclusion, restricted feature positions, chunk-size invariance, native-category handling, reference prefix scores, final-label isolation, paired bootstrap symmetry, insufficient-cluster behavior and self-contained hosted packaging. These checks establish the tested numerical and workflow properties. They do not establish statistical superiority or point-in-time correctness of anonymous provider features.

Remaining decisions: the research lead must assess the completed tuned-baseline results and the size and direction of uncertainty. the maintainer must choose the acceptable recall and false-alarm tradeoff. The account owner must enable the independent payment-fraud dataset if that source is to be used. The research lead must complete the closest-method comparison before proposing a novelty claim.
